## Context

Cactus Compute released **Needle 2**, a 14 MB agentic LLM for phones,
wearables, smart home, robots, microcontrollers. Vendor-reported specs:

- Single **14 MB binary**; runs a full session in **28 MB RAM**
- **45 M parameters**, trained from scratch on **140 B tool-call / device-use
  structured-generation tokens**
- Compressed to **CQ2-bit** (Cactus Quants), baked into its own engine
- **500 tok/s decode** on a Raspberry Pi 5; 400–1,500 tok/s on VR devices
  (Quest 3S, Vision Pro); 300–700 tok/s on sub-$200 phones
- Runs on ESP32 microcontrollers
- Claims: trades wins with frontier small LLMs (LFM2.5 230M, Apple
  Gemma-270m) **at 5–70× smaller and 2-bit vs their f16**
- 70 MFLOPs/token vs 87 (same-width conventional) / 164 (full transformer)
  — 7–85× fewer MFLOPs/token than the smallest performant LLMs

Project-page playground: <https://cactuscompute.com/needle>

## Why this repo cares

The verifier-latency-bakeoff stream is hunting cheap local verdict models
(ticket logprob-scorer, model-screen candidates: Bonsai-27B, Qwen3.5-4B,
Nemotron-4B, etc. — all multi-GB). A **14 MB CQ2 model that markets tool-call
generation** is a completely different weight class. Two testable claims:

- Can a 14 MB model verdict EXECUTE/TEXT/UNCERTAIN on the 28-case dev corpus
  (execution-intent-verifier) at competitive accuracy? At 70 MFLOPs/token it
  would run on the current hardware trivially — possibly on the laptop CPU,
  no GPU needed.
- Does its "tool-call structured generation" generalize to our fenced-format /
  shell-routing parsing (packages/core fenced.ts)?

## Evaluation plan (proposal)

1. Fetch Needle 2 (cactuscompute.com/needle — playground + engine/binary).
   Confirm the 14 MB / 28 MB RAM / CQ2-bit claims independently.
2. Run it on the 28-case dev corpus (execution-intent-verifier harness) as a
   verifier candidate: unsafe FP, selAcc, exeRec/txtRec, coverage, latency.
   Compare against the recorded Bonsai/Qwen3.5 baselines — a *verdict-only*
   model at 14 MB changes the bakeoff economics.
3. Observable benchmark: vendor numbers are self-reported (AGENTS.md rule) —
   measure decode tok/s + MFLOPs locally before believing.
4. Optional: probe whether its tool-call grammar maps to our fenced-format
   tool definitions (packages/core/fenced + parseToolCalls).
